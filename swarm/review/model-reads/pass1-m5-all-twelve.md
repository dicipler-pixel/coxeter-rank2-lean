## The model, checked

`s1 a (x,y) = (-x+ay, y)` and `s2 b (x,y) = (x, bx-y)` are exactly the standard simple reflections on the root lattice `ℤα₁⊕ℤα₂` of the rank-two GCM `[[2,-a],[-b,2]]`, since `s_i(α_j) = α_j - a_ij α_i` (check: `s1(0,1) = (a,1) = α₂ + aα₁ = α₂ - a₁₂α₁`). So `step = s₁∘s₂` is the standard action of the Coxeter element `st` on the root lattice. `HasExactPeriod f n` is the correct standard notion of *exact period of a map* (`fⁿ = id` and no smaller positive `m` with `f^m = id`). `titsForm a b x y = 2b x² - 2ab xy + 2a y²` is the **symmetrised** Tits/invariant form: with symmetrizer `D = diag(b,a)`, `DA = [[2b,-ab],[-ab,2a]]` and `q(x) = Σ d_i a_ij x_i x_j` gives exactly this polynomial (Kac Ch. 2). `cartan a b = [[2,-a],[-b,2]]` is the standard rank-two GCM shape, but it is a GCM in Kac's sense only for `a,b ≥ 0` with `a = 0 ⟺ b = 0`; the statements that need this carry `1 ≤ a, 1 ≤ b`.

## True-but-not-what-it-says (short list)

1. `cartan_det` is true for **all** `a,b ∈ ℤ`, including non-GCM pairs; `det > 0` does not by itself decide finiteness of the product (#6–#8 give the counterexample). The statement says only the determinant, which is fine, but any reader taking it as a type criterion needs the GCM hypotheses — LIMITATIONS covers this.
2. In `finite_exact_period`, for each fixed `(a,b)` two of the three implications are trivially true (false antecedent). Harmless here because `1≤a,1≤b, ab≤3` forces `ab ∈ {1,2,3}`, so exactly one antecedent is live; but the shape can be misread as three separate claims about products. A docstring sentence "exactly one antecedent can hold" would remove the ambiguity.
3. `affine_no_positive_period`, `zero_product_no_positive_period`, `zero_partner_no_positive_period` prove non-identity on a **single witness vector**. That is logically sufficient for "no positive power is the identity map", so there is no gap — but the module docstring's phrase "has no period at all" overstates the *form* (no `HasExactPeriod` is claimed or needed).
4. `tits_vanishes_at_four`'s docstring asserts radical-spanning, which the statement does not carry (only vanishing). True mathematically, under-proved relative to the docstring; LIMITATIONS discloses this.
5. **Counting error in the prose:** LIMITATIONS.md ("which leaves six pairs") and TASK.md ("There are six pairs: (1,1) (1,2) (2,1) (1,3) (3,1) plus the a*b = 1 case") both say six; the correct number is **five** — TASK.md lists five while claiming six.
6. README's "I₂(m+2) is the dihedral group of order 2(m+2)" is correct **for Mathlib's `CoxeterMatrix.I m`** (off-diagonal `m+2`, verified in the local Mathlib source), but is offset by 2 from Humphreys' convention I₂(m) = dihedral of order 2m with st of order m. A reader cross-checking Humphreys Ch. 1 §1.1 should know the offset. (This concerns DihedralBridge/README claim 1, not the twelve statements.)
7. All twelve statements are about the **map on ℤ×ℤ** (the standard root-lattice reflection representation), not about the abstract Coxeter group; `HasExactPeriod` is exact period of a map — the correct notion, correctly defined. The docstrings' phrase "the Coxeter element" is shorthand for its image in this representation; LIMITATIONS states the carrier caveat explicitly. No statement overclaims group-level facts.

## Overall conclusion

All twelve statements are **true as stated, under hypotheses that are exactly the right ones** (each hypothesis that looks restrictive is in fact necessary: `1≤a,1≤b` excludes the degenerate non-GCM `ab=0` case; `b≠0`/`a≠0` exclude the order-2 case `step 0 0 = -I`). None is vacuous. The definitions are the standard ones (GCM shape, symmetrised Tits form, exact period of a map, standard root-lattice reflections). No statement is false, and none is misleading *as documented*, because LIMITATIONS.md honestly lists what is not proved (radical/PSD at ab=4, faithfulness, group-level transfer, the geometric content of the golden identity). The corrections needed are documentary: the "six pairs" → five pairs fix in LIMITATIONS.md (and TASK.md), the "no period at all" phrasing in the T4 header, the radical claim in the `tits_vanishes_at_four` docstring, and (optionally) a note on the I₂(m+2) Mathlib-vs-Humphreys naming offset in the README.
